SAFETY CONCERN: All three medications are serotonergic. The combination of an SSRI (sertraline) + opioid with serotonergic activity (tramadol) + triptan (sumatriptan) places the patient at significant risk for SEROTONIN SYNDROME (Hunter Criteria).

Assessment and Plan
1. Depression — Stable on sertraline 100mg. Continue current dose.
2. Migraine — Sumatriptan PRN effective. However, concurrent use with sertraline increases serotonin syndrome risk. Consider non-serotonergic alternative (e.g., ubrogepant, rimegepant).
3. Pain management — Tramadol prescribed by outside provider. CRITICAL: Tramadol has serotonergic properties and combined with sertraline creates HIGH risk of serotonin syndrome. Recommend switching to non-serotonergic analgesic (acetaminophen, NSAIDs, or non-tramadol opioid if needed).
4. Patient education — Counsel on symptoms of serotonin syndrome: agitation, confusion, rapid heart rate, high blood pressure, dilated pupils, muscle twitching, diarrhea. Seek immediate medical attention if symptoms occur.
